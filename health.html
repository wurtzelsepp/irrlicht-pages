Health & safety

Sensitivity to light (photosensitive epilepsy)

A very small percentage of people may experience epileptic seizures or loss of awareness when exposed to certain light patterns, flashes or rapid changes between light and dark, such as those that occur on screens and in video games. This can also affect people with no history of epilepsy.

If you or anyone in your family has epilepsy or has had seizures, consult a doctor before playing.

Stop playing immediately and consult a doctor if you notice any of the following while playing: dizziness, altered vision, eye or muscle twitching, involuntary movements, disorientation, loss of awareness or convulsions. Parents should supervise children while they play and watch for these signs.

Reducing the risk

  • Play in a well-lit room and keep a distance from the screen.
  • Do not play when you are tired or short of sleep.
  • Take a break of at least 10 to 15 minutes every hour.
  • Under "Options > Access", "Reduce motion" turns off flicker, pulsing and bobbing.

Motion and nausea

Camera flights can cause dizziness or nausea in some people. If this happens, take a break.

Surroundings and traffic

Do not play while walking, driving, cycling or in any other situation in which you need to pay attention to your surroundings.

Posture, eyes and hands

Playing for long periods can tire your eyes, hands, wrists or arms. Take regular breaks. If discomfort persists, stop playing and consult a doctor.

Volume

Keep the volume at a reasonable level when using headphones. Listening at high volume for long periods can damage your hearing.
